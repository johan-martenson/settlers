# SETTLERS II COMBAT SYSTEM SPECIFICATION

## Status

This document describes the combat system of Settlers II as accurately
as currently understood.

## Definitions

Military Building
Barracks, Guardhouse, Watchtower, Fortress (and to some degree Headquarters).

Attacker
Soldier participating in an attack against an enemy military building.

Defender
Soldier defending an attacked military building.

Garrison
Soldiers currently stationed inside a military building.

Combat Slot
A simultaneous one-on-one fight between an attacker and a defender.

## Overview

Combat in Settlers II is building-centric rather than unit-centric.

The player never directly commands military units.

The player can only:

* Select an enemy military building.
* Choose how many soldiers to commit.
* Select weak attackers or strong attackers.

Combat consists of:

* Soldiers traveling to the target.
* Defenders being gathered.
* Multiple simultaneous one-on-one duels.
* Capture or defense of the military building.

## Attack Preconditions

A building may be attacked only if:

* It belongs to an enemy.
* It is a military building.
* It is constructed and has been occupied.
* It is within attack range of the player's territory.
* At least one friendly military building can provide attackers.

## Starting an Attack

1. Player selects an enemy military building.
2. Game determines how many attackers are available.
3. Player chooses the number of attackers and weak or strong attackers.
4. Eligible soldiers are selected automatically.
5. Selected soldiers leave their military buildings.
6. Soldiers physically walk toward the target.

The originating military building immediately loses those soldiers.

## Attacker Selection

Attackers are selected automatically.

Selection is influenced by:

* Distance to the target.
* Military settings.
* Soldier availability.

The player does not choose individual soldiers.

## Movement

Soldiers travel physically across the map. They walk both on regular roads and off roads which ever is closest.

During movement:

* Soldiers may be attacked by nobody.
* Soldiers do not fight until reaching the target.
* Soldiers do not heal.
* Current health is preserved.

Attackers may arrive at different times due to path length and traffic.

## Attacker Placements

An attacker will go to the target building's flag and wait for the first defender from the target building's garrison
to come out to defend it in combat. The other attackers will either:

1) cluster around the flag, and wait for defenders to fight, or
2) go directly to meet defenders from other military buildings nearby to fight them.

## Defender Selection

The target building's own garrison defends against attackers that walk up to its flag.

Additional defenders may be requested from nearby military buildings.

A military building may provide defenders only if:

* It belongs to the defending player.
* It is within military support range.
* It contains soldiers above its required minimum garrison.

Buildings do not normally empty themselves completely.

## Defender Selection Priority

Defender source buildings appear to be chosen primarily by distance.

Approximate priority:

1. Closest military building.
2. Next closest military building.
3. Next closest military building.

There is no evidence that rank influences building selection.

## Defender Dispatch

Selected defenders:

* Leave their source building.
* Walk to the attacked building.
* Join combat when they arrive.

Defenders may arrive after combat has already started.

## Defender Reinforcement Logic

Combat does not appear to use fixed reinforcement waves.

Instead, the game continuously evaluates defensive strength.

Conceptually:

```
while attack active:
    evaluate defenders needed
    dispatch additional defenders if required
```

Additional defenders may be requested when:

* A defender dies.
* Too few defenders are available.
* New attackers arrive.
* Existing defenders are already occupied in combat.

Defenders already travelling toward the target count as pending
reinforcements and should not trigger duplicate requests.

## Combat Structure

Combat consists of multiple simultaneous one-on-one duels.

Example:

```
Attacker A vs Defender A
Attacker B vs Defender B
Attacker C vs Defender C
```

Several duels may occur at the same time.

Combat is not formation-based.

Combat is not squad-based.

## Combat Slots

Each combat slot contains:

* One attacker.
* One defender.

When a soldier dies:

* The slot becomes vacant.
* Another available soldier may enter the slot.

Combat continues until one side has no soldiers remaining.

## Soldier Ranks

Soldiers have ranks:

* Private
* Private First Class
* Sergeant
* Officer
* General

Rank affects:

* Maximum health.
* Combat effectiveness.

## Soldier Health

Each soldier has:

* Rank
* Current health

Health persists between battles.

Health persists while travelling.

Health persists after promotion.

## Maximum Health

Private             3 HP
Private First Class 4 HP
Sergeant            5 HP
Officer             6 HP
General             7 HP

## Combat Resolution

Combat occurs in rounds.

Each round:

1. One soldier attacks.
2. Attack may hit or miss.
3. Successful hit removes 1 HP.
4. Combat continues.

A duel ends when one soldier reaches 0 HP.

## Winner Behavior

The winner survives with remaining health.

Example:

```
General starts with 7 HP
Takes 5 damage
Wins duel
```

Result:

```
General remains alive with 2 HP
```

The soldier keeps this health value.

The soldier does not automatically heal after victory.

## Successive Duels

A surviving soldier may participate in additional duels.

The soldier enters subsequent duels with current remaining health.

Example:

```
Duel 1:
    7 HP -> 3 HP

Duel 2:
    Starts at 3 HP
```

Health does not reset between duels.

## Promotion

Promotion changes rank.

Promotion does NOT heal the soldier.

Example:

```
Sergeant
Current HP = 1
Max HP = 5
```

Promoted to Officer

Result:

```
Current HP = 1
Max HP = 6
```

## Health Recovery

Known behavior:

* Soldiers heal while stationed in military buildings.
* Soldiers do not heal while travelling.
* Soldiers do not heal during combat.
* Promotion does not heal.

Exact healing timing is not fully documented.

A likely implementation is:

```
periodically:
    health = min(maxHealth, health + 1)
```

## Building Capture

If all defenders are defeated:

1. Defending force collapses.
2. Final surviving attacker becomes occupier.
3. Building changes ownership.
4. Territory is recalculated.

The building immediately becomes part of the attacker's military network.

## Building Defense Success

If all attackers are defeated:

1. Attack ends.
2. Building remains under defender ownership.
3. Surviving defenders remain alive.
4. Surviving defenders keep remaining health.

## Territory Recalculation

After a successful capture:

* Territory ownership is recalculated.
* Borders move.
* Enemy structures outside valid territory may collapse.
* Roads may become disconnected.

## Important Design Principles

The combat system should be modeled as:

```
Buildings fighting over territory
```

rather than:

```
Units fighting over units
```

Key properties:

* Building-centric.
* Persistent soldier health.
* Persistent soldier ranks.
* Multiple simultaneous duels.
* Physical soldier movement.
* Dynamic reinforcements.
* Territory-based conquest.

These characteristics are what make Settlers II combat feel distinct
from conventional RTS combat systems.
